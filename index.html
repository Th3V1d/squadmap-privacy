Squadmap Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 August 2026  ·  Effective: 6 August 2026

Squadmap is a group trip-planning app. You create a “squad” with friends and plan a trip together — dates, a route, where you're staying, shared expenses, photos — and optionally share your live location with that squad while you're travelling.

This policy explains exactly what data Squadmap collects, why, who can see it, and what control you have over it. We've tried to write it in plain language rather than legalese.

2. The short version

  • Squadmap collects the data you give it: your email, a nickname, and the trip content you create (squads, dates, routes, accommodation, expenses, photos).
  • If — and only if — you switch it on, Squadmap collects your live location and shares it with one squad. This continues in the background until you switch it off.
  • Your data is visible to the people you choose: your friends and the members of your squads. It is not public and it is not sold.
  • We do not use any analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting services. There is no advertising identifier, no tracking across apps or websites, and no third-party ad SDK in this app.
  • Squadmap has no payment processing. The expense feature is a shared notepad for splitting costs; no money moves through the app and no payment card details are ever collected.
  • You can delete your account and its data from inside the app at any time.

3.2 Your trip content

Everything you enter into a squad is stored so your squadmates can see it:

  • Squads — squad name, who created it, the member list and each member's role (owner or member)
  • Trip dates — start and end dates
  • Route — map waypoints (latitude/longitude) for planned stops
  • Accommodation — name, address, check-in/check-out dates, a booking link, and free-text notes
  • Expenses — a description, an amount, who paid, and how it is split between members, plus whether each share is marked settled
  • Photos — images you choose to upload to a squad gallery or attach to an accommodation

Please note: the notes, descriptions, and address fields are free text. Whatever you type into them is stored and shown to your squadmates — so avoid putting sensitive information (booking reference numbers, passport details, payment details) in them.

Legal basis: performance of a contract — this content is the service.

3.3 Location data

This is the most sensitive data Squadmap handles, so we want to be precise about it.

What happens when you turn on “Share my location” for a squad:

  • Squadmap asks iOS for location permission. To keep sharing working while the app is in the background, it asks for the “Always” permission. If you only grant “While Using the App,” sharing still works, but only while Squadmap is open.
  • Your device records your position at roughly 100-metre accuracy, and sends an update each time you move about 25 metres.
  • Each update — your latitude and longitude — is sent to our server and stored. We keep one current position per squad, per person; each update overwrites the last. We do not build or retain a history of everywhere you have been.
  • Your position is visible only to the members of that one squad, live, while sharing is on.
  • Only one squad at a time. Turning sharing on for a new squad automatically turns it off for the previous one.

When you turn sharing off: Squadmap stops collecting your location immediately. However, your last known position is not deleted — it is marked as no longer live, and your squadmates continue to see it as a “last seen” marker. This is deliberate, so a squad doesn't lose track of someone whose phone died. If you want that last position removed, delete your account (Section 8) or ask us (Section 9).

How to turn it off:

  • In the app: the “Share my location” toggle on the squad's Route screen.
  • On your device: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Squadmap, where you can revoke the permission entirely at any time.

Squadmap never collects your location unless you have explicitly turned sharing on for a squad.

Legal basis: consent. You may withdraw it at any time using either method above.

3.4 Photos

When you upload a photo, you pick it using the standard iOS photo picker. Squadmap never gets access to your photo library — iOS hands over only the specific images you select, which is why the app never asks for photo library permission.

Photos are uploaded to our storage provider and are visible to the members of the squad you uploaded them to. Uploaded photos are resized and re-encoded before upload, which removes embedded metadata such as the GPS coordinates that cameras attach to photos.

Files are stored under unguessable random identifiers. While the storage location is technically reachable by direct link, links are only ever shown inside the app to members of that squad. Please treat squad photos as visible to your squad, not as private.

Legal basis: consent.

3.5 Friends

You send a friend request by entering someone's email address. Squadmap checks whether an account exists for that address and, if so, shows you that person's nickname and avatar so you can confirm it's the right person. The email address itself is never revealed to the person searching — you have to already know it.

We store the friendship link between the two accounts and its status (pending or accepted). Either person can remove it at any time. Friendships are visible only to the two people involved.

Legal basis: performance of a contract.

3.6 Calendar

If you tap “Add to Apple Calendar,” Squadmap asks for write-only calendar permission and adds your trip as an event. Write-only means the app is technically incapable of reading anything already in your calendar. The event is created on your device; nothing is sent to us.

If you tap “Add to Google Calendar,” your browser opens a Google Calendar link with the squad name and trip dates in it. This is a normal web link, not an integration — from that point Google's privacy policy applies.

Legal basis: consent.

3.7 Anti-abuse (hCaptcha)

Our sign-in and sign-up screens show an hCaptcha challenge to stop automated bots creating accounts. hCaptcha is operated by Intuition Machines, Inc. and, in order to distinguish humans from bots, receives your IP address and signals about your device and how you interact with the challenge.

This happens on the sign-in screen before you have an account, and we cannot switch it off — it protects every user of the service. See the hCaptcha privacy policy.

Legal basis: legitimate interest — protecting the service and its users from automated abuse and spam account creation.

3.8 Technical data

Like any app that talks to a server, your device's IP address is visible to our hosting provider as part of delivering the service, and appears in standard server logs. We do not use it to build a profile of you and we do not use it for advertising.

4. What Squadmap does not collect

To be explicit, this app contains no:

  • Analytics or usage-tracking SDK (no Google Analytics, Firebase, Mixpanel, Amplitude, or similar)
  • Advertising SDK, ad network, or advertising identifier (IDFA). Squadmap does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission because it does not track you.
  • Crash-reporting or telemetry SDK (no Crashlytics, Sentry, or similar)
  • Payment processing of any kind. The expense splitter is a manual ledger — marking a share “settled” is just a note; no money moves through Squadmap and no card or bank details are ever collected.
  • Push notifications
  • Access to your contacts, microphone, camera roll, health data, or Bluetooth
  • Any collection of data about you from third-party sources

The app's only code dependency is the client library for our hosting provider.

6. Where your data is stored and how it's transferred

Your data is stored on Supabase infrastructure in the European Union (Ireland).

If data is transferred outside the UK/EEA, that transfer is covered by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses as incorporated in our provider's data processing agreement.

All traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted in transit using HTTPS. Live location updates travel over an encrypted realtime connection (WebSocket). Data is encrypted at rest by our hosting provider. Access to your data is enforced at the database level by row-level security rules, so the server itself refuses to return data belonging to squads you are not a member of.

No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

7. How long we keep it

  • While your account exists: your account data and trip content are kept so the service works.
  • Location: only your single most recent position per squad is stored. It is overwritten by each new update and does not accumulate into a location history.
  • When you leave a squad: you immediately lose access to it, and it disappears from your app. Content you contributed to that squad (expenses you logged, photos you uploaded) stays with the squad for the remaining members.
  • When you delete your account: see Section 8.

8. Deleting your account and your data

You can delete your account from inside the app: Account → Delete Account. You'll be asked to confirm, because it cannot be undone.

Deleted immediately and permanently:

  • Your login credentials and email
  • Your profile and nickname
  • Your friendships
  • Your stored locations, including any “last seen” positions
  • Your squad memberships
  • Your expenses and expense shares
  • Photos you uploaded, including the image files themselves

What remains: squads, routes, accommodation entries, and photo records that you created are kept for the other members of those squads, but your name is permanently removed from them — they become unattributed. This is so deleting your account doesn't destroy a trip other people are still relying on. If you need this content removed too, contact us and we'll take care of it.

You can also email david.szasz111@gmail.com to request deletion instead of doing it in the app.
